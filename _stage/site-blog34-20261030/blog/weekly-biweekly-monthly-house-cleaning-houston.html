Weekly vs. Biweekly vs. Monthly House Cleaning in Houston: How to Choose

Pick a schedule by how fast your house gets dirty again. Here's what each recurring visit covers, who each schedule fits and how to tell when you've picked the wrong one.

Donta & Kenia, Puretek Global Cleaning
October 30, 2026 · 5 min read
The short answer

Weekly fits busy households with kids or pets. Biweekly (every two weeks) fits most Houston homes and is where most households start. Monthly fits small homes with one or two adults who keep up between visits.

What does a recurring cleaning visit cover?

A recurring clean is maintenance. The crew works through the same list every visit, so the rooms that get used every day never fall behind. At Puretek Global Cleaning that list covers kitchens, bathrooms and living areas, plus dusting, vacuuming and mopping throughout the house.

What it doesn't cover is the slow build-up inside the oven, fridge and microwave and along the baseboards. That belongs to a deep clean. The frequency you choose decides how much daily mess piles up between visits. It doesn't replace the occasional deep clean.

Where does the difference between schedules show up?

Every schedule handles the same rooms. The difference is how much has built up by the time the crew arrives. These are the four places we see it first.

01

The kitchen

In NSF International's 2011 household germ study, microbiologists had 22 families swab 30 everyday surfaces. Coliform bacteria turned up on more than 75% of dish sponges and rags and on 45% of kitchen sinks. That's a small study, but it lines up with what we see. The kitchen falls behind first. NSF found the areas where food is prepared carried significantly more bacteria than the rest of the house.

If you cook most nights, the counters, stovetop, sink and floor in front of the stove are the best reason to clean weekly. Swap or sanitize your sponge between visits, whatever schedule you choose.

02

Bathrooms

Soap scum and hard-water spots get harder to remove the longer they sit. On a weekly or biweekly schedule they come off with a normal scrub. On a monthly schedule they can need more time and stronger products, which is why monthly clients do best with a quick wipe of the sink and shower between visits.

In the same NSF study, 27% of them carried coliform bacteria. Rinse yours out when you rinse the sink.

03

Floors and dust

The EPA reports that Americans spend about 90% of their time indoors, where the concentrations of some pollutants are often 2 to 5 times higher than typical outdoor levels. Dust on floors, ceiling fans and vents is part of that indoor load, and regular vacuuming and dusting is how a schedule keeps it down. Houston adds its own load: oak pollen every spring coats sills, ceiling fans and patio-door tracks, which is a good reason to step up to weekly in March and April.

Pets that shed and kids who play on the floor are the clearest signs a home needs weekly vacuuming and mopping. Homes without either usually do fine on biweekly.

04

The things no schedule catches

Inside the oven, fridge and microwave and along the baseboards, grime builds up slowly no matter how often the crew visits. That's why recurring service starts with a one-time initial deep clean. It resets the house so every visit after it is maintenance.

Most homes on a recurring schedule benefit from another deep clean every few months, or before hosting family for the holidays.

Houston tip: the EPA says indoor relative humidity should be kept below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. Houston's outdoor humidity runs well above that for much of the year, and damp bathrooms and closets are where mildew starts. If you're on a monthly schedule, run the bathroom fan during and after every shower and keep the AC running when you travel. Mildew that has a full month to grow is harder to remove.

How can you tell your cleaning schedule isn't working?

  • The house looks dirty again within a few days of every visit. Move up one step, monthly to biweekly or biweekly to weekly.
  • Every visit runs long because the crew is catching up instead of maintaining.
  • Soap scum or mildew keeps coming back in the same shower or closet.
  • Pet hair is back on the floors the day after a visit.
  • The house still looks clean when the crew arrives. You may be able to step down a level.
  • Your household changed: a new baby, a new pet, a new work-from-home job or a kid home for the summer.

Questions we get

How often should a house be professionally cleaned?

For most Houston households, every two weeks. Homes with young kids, pets that shed or heavy daily cooking usually need weekly service. Small homes with one or two adults often do fine with monthly visits.

Do I need a deep clean before recurring service starts?

Yes. Recurring service starts with a one-time initial deep clean that covers baseboards and the inside of the oven, fridge and microwave. After that, each visit maintains the house instead of catching it up.

Can I change my schedule later?

Yes. You can start biweekly and adjust after a month or two once they see how the house holds up between visits.

Do you bring your own supplies?

Yes, we bring everything needed. If you prefer specific products in your home, let us know.
